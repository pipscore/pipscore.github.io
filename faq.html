How to Use PipScore

Getting a good reading, and answers to common questions.

← Back to PipScore

The three steps

  1. Fan your hand so each card's top-left corner is visible. PipScore reads the corner of each card — the rank and the suit symbol. Overlap the cards, but leave every corner showing.
  2. Tap Take Photo. Your phone's own camera app opens. Frame the fan, take the picture, and accept it.
  3. Read your total. PipScore identifies each card and shows the point total, along with every card it found.
The one tip that matters most: every card's corner must be visible in the photo. If a corner is covered by the card in front of it, that card cannot be read. Spread the fan a little wider than feels natural — a gap of roughly a fingertip's width between corners works well.

Getting a good reading

How should I hold the cards?

Fan them so the corners step evenly across, like a staircase. Hold them flat-on to the camera rather than tilted away, and keep the whole fan inside the frame. Cards laid out on a table work just as well as a hand held up.

How close should I be?

Close enough that the fan roughly fills the frame, but far enough that the corners are sharp and in focus. If the photo is blurry, the corners cannot be read. Tap your camera's screen to focus before taking the shot.

What about lighting?

Normal room light is usually fine. The two things that cause trouble are glare — a lamp or window reflecting off the glossy card surface — and shadow, where your own hand or phone darkens the corners. If a reading fails, move slightly so the light is coming from behind you rather than in front, or use your camera app's flash.

Does the background matter?

A plain, contrasting surface helps. A dark tablecloth or felt makes white cards stand out. A busy patterned surface makes it harder.

When something goes wrong

It says no cards were detected.

Almost always one of four things: a corner was covered by another card, the photo was blurry, glare washed out the corners, or the fan was too far away. Re-spread the fan so every corner shows, tap to focus, and try again.

It missed one card in my hand.

That card's corner was probably covered, shadowed, or cut off at the edge of the frame. Widen the fan slightly and re-take the photo.

It read a card as the wrong rank or suit.

This can happen with worn cards, unusual card designs, strong glare, or a soft-focus photo. Re-taking the photo with the fan spread wider and better focus usually fixes it. A future update will let you correct a card directly in the app.

My deck has big corner numbers and it doesn't work well.

PipScore is built for standard playing cards. Jumbo-index decks — the ones with oversized corner numbers and suit symbols — may not read correctly, because the corners look quite different from a standard deck. We are looking at this.

Setting point values

How do I match my game's scoring?

Use the dials on the main screen to set what an Ace, King, Queen, Jack, and Ten are worth. Number cards always score their face value — a 7 is 7 points. Set the dials before you take the photo, and the total will use your values.

Do my point values stay set?

Not yet. Point values return to their defaults each time you open PipScore, so set them at the start of a session. Saving your preset between sessions is planned for a future update.

Can I use it for a game that isn't listed?

Yes. PipScore does not know the rules of any particular game — it reads the cards and adds up the values you set. Any game where you total the points in a hand will work.

Scores and history

Does it keep a record of my hands?

While the app is open, PipScore keeps a list of your recent hands so you can look back at earlier scores in the same session. This list is held in memory only — it is cleared when you close the app, and nothing is written to storage.

Does it keep a running total across hands?

PipScore totals the points in each hand you photograph. It does not add hands together into a game-long score or track players — you keep the running score the way you normally would.

Privacy and permissions

Why does it open my regular camera app?

By design. PipScore hands the job to the camera app already on your phone instead of running a camera itself. That is why it needs no camera permission at all — and it means you get your phone's own focus, flash, and zoom.

What permissions does PipScore ask for?

None. PipScore requests no permissions whatsoever — not camera, not storage, not network access.

Where do my photos go?

The photo is held in PipScore's private app storage only as long as it takes to read the cards, then deleted. It is never uploaded, never added to your gallery by PipScore, and never shared. Note that your phone's own camera app may keep its own copy in your gallery, as it does for any photo — that copy is outside PipScore's control, and you can delete it like any other picture.

Does it work without internet?

Yes, entirely. PipScore has no internet access at all — everything happens on your phone. It works in airplane mode, on a plane, or anywhere with no signal. Full details are in the Privacy Policy.
